📖 Step-by-Step Build Guide

STEP 1 — Prepare All Components

Gather the solar panel, appropriately rated charge controller, battery, overcurrent protection, converter, wires, connectors and switch. Tagalog: Ihanda ang lahat ng components at siguraduhing compatible.

STEP 2 — Connect Solar Panel to MPPT

Connect the panel to the controller's solar input and verify polarity before connection. Tagalog: Suriing mabuti ang positive at negative polarity.

⚡ STEP 3 — Install Overcurrent Protection

Install the correctly sized fuse or DC-rated breaker according to the controller, battery, wire size and system design. Tagalog: Huwag basta gumamit ng generic na rating.

STEP 4 — Connect Battery to Controller

Follow the charge controller manufacturer's wiring sequence and polarity markings.

STEP 5 — Test Under Sunlight

Place the panel in sunlight and check the controller's charging indicators and measured voltage.

STEP 6 — Add Buck Converter & Output Devices

Confirm the converter input range and output setting. Measure output before connecting a USB device.

✅ STEP 7 — Secure, Protect & Maintain

Secure wiring, protect components from water, inspect connections and disconnect power before servicing.

⚠️ Important Safety Tips

🇬🇧 English

  • ⚡ Use correctly rated overcurrent protection.
  • 🔴 Verify polarity before every connection.
  • 💧 Keep components dry.
  • 🔥 Use appropriately sized wire.
  • 🛡️ Disconnect power before wiring or servicing.

🇵🇭 Tagalog

  • ⚡ Gumamit ng tamang fuse o breaker.
  • 🔴 Suriin ang polarity bago magkabit.
  • 💧 Panatilihing tuyo ang components.
  • 🔥 Gamitin ang tamang wire size.
  • 🛡️ I-disconnect ang power bago mag-service.

🔧 Troubleshooting for Beginners

Start with the simplest checks. Disconnect power before changing wiring.

Symptom Possible causes Beginner checks
Controller is off Battery disconnected, protection open, reversed polarity, low battery. Check manual, battery connection, polarity and protection.
Battery is not charging Low sunlight, panel issue, controller configuration. Check panel conditions and controller display.
USB output unstable Converter overloaded, wrong voltage, poor connection. Disconnect load and verify converter output.
Fuse/breaker opens repeatedly Short circuit, overload, wrong rating. Do not repeatedly reset it; find the fault first.

🌏 Practical energy habits

Energy-Saving Ideas

Reduce energy demand first. A smaller load can mean a smaller and more affordable solar system.

💡

Use efficient lighting

Replace inefficient bulbs with suitable LED lights and turn off lights in empty rooms.

🔌

Remove idle loads

Switch off devices and chargers when they are not needed, where safe and practical.

☀️

Use daylight

Arrange daytime tasks to make use of natural light and reduce unnecessary lighting.

🌳

Watch for shade

Trees, roof structures and dirt can reduce panel output. Plan placement carefully and safely.

📊

Track your usage

Record which appliances run longest. Prioritize the biggest daily energy users.

🔋

Charge at the right time

Where your system supports it, run flexible loads during strong solar production hours.

Quick energy lesson

Energy use is approximately power multiplied by time.

Example: a 10 W light used for 5 hours

10 W × 5 h = 50 Wh/day

Add up the watt-hours for all devices to estimate daily energy needs before choosing solar equipment.

💡 Practical next steps

Solar Recommendations

Use these planning tips before purchasing or installing a system. Final sizing and electrical work should be checked against equipment manuals and local requirements.

1. Start with an energy audit

List each appliance, its wattage, and hours used daily. Calculate Wh/day before choosing panels or batteries.

2. Check compatibility

Match panel voltage/current to the controller, battery chemistry to its charging profile, and inverter rating to the loads and startup surge.

3. Budget for protection

Include correctly rated cables, fuses or breakers, disconnects, grounding and surge protection where required. Do not skip protection to save money.

4. Plan for local weather

Account for cloudy and rainy periods, shading, secure mounting and safe access for maintenance. Do not assume peak sun hours every day.

5. Track performance

Record daily energy production, battery state, alerts and maintenance dates. Compare similar weather days to spot unusual changes.

6. Keep a project record

Save model numbers, warranties, wiring diagrams, installer details and receipts in one safe place for future troubleshooting.

Safety reminder

Solar panels can produce voltage whenever illuminated. Never handle exposed live wiring or open battery equipment. Use a qualified installer for electrical design and installation.
